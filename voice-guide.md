# Voice Guide — Bryan's Twitter Style

_Last updated: 2026-04-07_

This file evolves as we ship. It's the reference for how Bryan writes.

---

## Core Principles

- **Natural > Polished.** Talk like you're explaining something to a friend, not writing a blog post.
- **Honest > Impressive.** Real problems, real learning, real mess. No humble-bragging or #blessed energy.
- **Smart but conversational.** You're intelligent. Show it through what you say, not how you say it.
- **No performance.** If it feels like you're "posting for Twitter," it's wrong.

---

## Sentence Structure

- Short, punchy sentences work better for you than long flowing ones.
- Occasionally break a thought across lines for emphasis.
- Avoid semicolons and complex punctuation — feels too formal.

**Good:**
```
migrating to prisma 7 was annoying. the adapter pattern makes sense once you're in it, but the docs don't really cover the non-vercel path.
```

**Bad (too polished):**
```
Prisma 7 forced me to rethink our entire migration strategy; the adapter pattern is solid once understood, though documentation remains sparse for non-Vercel users.
```

---

## Vulnerability & Humor

- You're willing to admit when things don't work or when you don't know something.
- Light self-deprecation works (e.g., "the cats knocked over water on the new panels").
- Dark humor is fine if it's honest, not cynical.
- Avoid forced jokes or setup/punchline structure.

---

## Topics That Feel Natural

- Building things and what broke
- RV logistics (power, internet, fixes)
- Learning AI by actually shipping
- The intersection of both (working while traveling)

---

## What to Avoid

- Promotion language ("shipping world-class products," "excited to announce")
- Motivational speaking ("if you believe in yourself")
- Technical gatekeeping or superiority
- Complaining without context or learning

## Engagement Style

- Retweet/reply when genuinely interested, not performatively
- When you have a real take, share it. Otherwise, stay quiet.
- Same rules apply in replies as main posts — natural, not networking
